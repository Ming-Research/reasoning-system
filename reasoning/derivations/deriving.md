Decision premises-only: A derivation names its premises and concludes only what they give, because coherence is that every decision follows from the axioms, the facts it cites and earlier decisions, and everyone reasons from the same premises in the same way.

Rests on: C:coherence, C:same-reasoning, D:rests-on

Decision trade-off-to-owner: Where the premises leave several workable approaches, the trade-off is put to the owner and the ruling becomes a derivation, instead of an agent settling it, because the decisions are the owner's input.

Rests on: C:owner-inputs

Decision uncovered-choices: A choice between workable approaches that no derivation covers is raised to be derived, instead of settled by whoever meets it, because such a choice is a decision, and the decisions are the owner's input.

Rests on: C:owner-inputs, D:trade-off-to-owner

Decision revise-not-work-around: When a question is better answered by changing an earlier derivation, the earlier derivation is revised, instead of being worked around, because the content must stay coherent through every revision, and a workaround leaves a derivation whose conclusion the later decisions no longer follow.

Rests on: C:coherence

Decision trade-offs-only: Only choices between workable approaches are recorded, and a rule shared by several derivations is written once on their common ancestor, because a choice with one workable approach is no trade-off and serves no coherence, and a rule written twice can drift apart.

Rests on: C:owner-inputs, C:membership, C:coherence
