Decision words-not-reasons: Nobody's words are a reason: what someone said is evidence of their reasoning, and the owner's input enters the records only as the constitution or a ruling, because every decision must follow from its premises, and the owner's inputs are the constitution and the decisions.

Rests on: C:coherence, C:owner-inputs

Rejected:
- Recording what the owner asked as the reason for a decision: rejected because words hold in their context and time, and a decision resting on them cannot be checked or derived again when the context is lost (C:coherence).

Decision ask-for-premise: When the reasoning behind the owner's words cannot be found in the records, the owner is asked for its premise, instead of it being guessed or the words cited in its place, because a conclusion without its premise is not coherent, and a premise the records cannot give can only come from the owner.

Rests on: C:coherence, C:owner-inputs, D:words-not-reasons

Decision point-out-contradiction: When the owner's words contradict a derivation, the contradiction is put to the owner with both sides and their premises; either the derivation stands, or it is revised by the owner's ruling, because no two contents may contradict, and changing a derivation is the owner's.

Rests on: C:coherence, C:owner-inputs, D:words-not-reasons
