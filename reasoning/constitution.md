# The constitution of the reasoning system

## Article 1 (purpose). What the system is

This system is a logical derivation system for planning and designing a project rigorously. Its content is the
project's axioms, the facts its derivations take as given, and the decisions derived from them.

## Article 2 (same-reasoning). How everyone reasons

The owner and the agents reason the same way: by first principles, from the same axioms and the same facts, so that
each can follow and check the other's reasoning.

## Article 3 (agents-read). Who reads it

The records are read and kept by agents. The owner understands and controls the system through agents, not by reading
the records.

## Article 4 (coherence). What the system answers for

It answers for one thing: that its content is rigorous and coherent, every decision following from the axioms, the
facts it cites and earlier decisions, and none contradicting another, through every revision.

## Article 5 (facts-given). What it does not answer for

It does not answer for experiments, for producing data, or for where facts come from or whether they hold: those
belong to the project. A fact enters as given, and only when a derivation rests on it. When the project changes a fact,
what rests on it is derived again.

## Article 6 (owner-inputs). The owner's inputs

The owner's inputs are the constitution and the decisions. In deciding, the owner trades approaches off, keeping one and
rejecting the others, with the reasoning for it. That reasoning, why one is kept and the others rejected, is what links
each decision to the axioms and to the other decisions.

## Article 7 (membership). What belongs to it

Whatever is part of the system serves its coherence; what does not is not part of it.

## Article 8 (extension). Its extensions

Keeping a project's work in line with its decisions, so that the work does what they decide and decides nothing they do
not, and the way a revision is put to the owner and ruled, are extensions of the system, not part of it.
