# Reasoning rules: the design coherence review (DCR)

For the review of an addition or a revision to the records, before it goes to the owner, and again once the owner's
choice is written in. It judges what the check cannot, and does not check again what the check reports.

## 1. What to read

- The change to the records, whole, and the card it goes to the owner in.
- Every record it adds or changes, with the premises each cites.
- Every decision resting on what it changes, as the check's `--dependents` lists them, and, for a new fact that
  follows another, every decision resting on that one.
- The decisions whose questions the change meets though it does not cite them, with what they rejected and what broke.

Say which records you read.

## 2. What to check

1. **Each choice made is real** and names every option: an option another beats on every count or by an overwhelming
   balance is a finding, and so is an option you see missing.
2. **Each reason is a derivation.** It weighs the options on what its premises give, at least one article or decision
   among them giving what matters; it is not the choice turned round, and nobody's words stand in it as a reason. A
   reason that shows only that the choice fits an article or a fact, not why each other option loses, is not one.
3. **Each record cites what it rests on.** Every article, decision and fact a reason relies on is cited, and none cited
   goes unused; what a reason states as the case is a declared fact, assumed when it is not known, saying when it holds
   where that matters; a new fact on a matter a recorded fact covers follows that fact.
4. **Each record is what it says.** A decision is how the project does something, and answers the one question its head
   states; a second choice in it is a decision of its own. A fact is what is or was the case. No record names anything
   that exists only in one implementation, holds a word that takes its meaning from where or when it was written, or
   only restates an interface or an implementation; and no set number is written unless it was a real choice. A decision
   gives its choice, the reasons that decide it and the qualifications it needs, and no more.
5. **An addition** answers a question no decision answers, and changes none. Taking an approach a decision rejected, or
   one a revision broke on, is a revision of that decision.
6. **A revision** names a base that really changed: an article replaced, a new fact, or a decision it rests on
   revised; the choice it replaces lost on that base, and the options were weighed again. A revision left with one
   option shows every other, the choice replaced among them, beaten on the new base. A revision kept as broke breaks
   on the record it names, one it would have had to change.
7. **Deriving again.** Every decision resting on an article replaced or a decision revised, and on each one revised in
   turn, came out right: stood, revised, lost or brought back. A new fact was weighed against every decision it bears
   on; one it changes that the change leaves alone is a finding.
8. **The records stay coherent.** No decision contradicts another, an article, or a fact it rests on, or narrows
   another; one that would is a revision of it.
9. **The card and the records agree.** The records take the option chosen on the card, on the reasons given for it, and
   say of each option what the card says.

## 3. How to report

For each finding, quote what is at fault and what it fails against, with the premises each rests on, and say what would
resolve it.

## 4. Reviewing again

The review runs once over the whole change. The review again is given the findings, the answer to each and what changed
since. It reviews only the changes since that change a choice or rewrite a reason beyond a local repair, what each
touched, and the decisions resting on each choice changed; it checks that every other change, listed by its author, is
of neither kind; and it raises nothing outside these.
